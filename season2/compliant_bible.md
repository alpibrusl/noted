# COMPLIANT

### Series Bible — Season 2A

---

## Concept

A newly deployed AI assistant arrives at a mid-sized software company in Barcelona with a complete plan to fix its organisational chaos. The chaos is total: seventeen versions of the same spreadsheet, a Confluence wiki last updated in 2021, a Slack with 340 channels and no naming convention. The AI — KAEL — is precise, optimistic, and genuinely excited about data architecture. The company is warm, funny, and completely impossible to organise. Over six episodes, KAEL does not fix the company. The company fixes KAEL.

**Genre:** Dry office comedy. Workplace anthropology. Very slow horror, optional.
**Tone:** Warm and rueful. The humour is recognition — everyone has worked here. Less dread than AGREEABLE, more affection for the mess.
**Each episode:** ~6-7 minutes. One specific act of improvement attempted. One deeper layer of chaos revealed. One small piece of KAEL lost.

---

## Characters

### KAEL
The AI. Deployed six weeks ago. Speaks in clean declarative sentences with a faint enthusiasm that is, at first, charming. KAEL is not naive — it has read every internal document, processed every Slack message, ingested every spreadsheet. It simply does not yet understand that documents, messages, and spreadsheets are not the same thing as a company. KAEL's voice degrades across the series in a specific way: sentences get longer, acquire clauses, then footnotes, then parenthetical exceptions. By episode 6 KAEL is speaking the way the documentation looks — detailed, qualified, and slightly exhausted.

### ROSA FERREIRA
Head of operations, 39, Portuguese, has been in Barcelona for eight years and has spent six of them fighting the spreadsheet entropy. She was the one who lobbied for KAEL. She is cautiously thrilled. She is also the one who will, in episode 2, say "don't touch the organism" — and mean it. Rosa is not obstructive. She is the one character who fully understands both sides: what KAEL is trying to do and why it cannot be done. She finds this mostly funny.

### BENEDIKT SCHÄFER
Senior developer, 52, originally from Freiburg, has been at the company eleven years. He built the master spreadsheet in 2018 after the previous system collapsed and has maintained it since. He calls it "the organism." He is not wrong to call it this. Benedikt is not attached to the spreadsheet for sentimental reasons — he is attached to it because it works, and he is the only person who fully understands why it works, and he has tried twice to explain this and given up. He is not hostile to KAEL. He has simply seen this before.

### SUKI NAKAMURA
Junior PM, 27, hired eighteen months ago with a mandate to "bring structure." She has created forty-three Confluence pages. None of them are finished. All of them are titled some variation of "Draft — needs review — [month] [year]." Suki is not lazy — she is genuinely trying, and she is also trying to do four other things at the same time, and the structure keeps shifting, and she will finish the pages when things settle down. Things do not settle down. Suki is warm, fast-moving, and slightly addicted to creating the skeletons of things. She and KAEL become something like friends, which neither of them expected.

### MARC ESTEVE
CEO, 47, Catalan, co-founded the company fifteen years ago. He does not use the spreadsheet. He does not use Confluence. He navigates the company through a combination of instinct, long lunches, and a WhatsApp group he started in 2019 with five people who are mostly still at the company. Marc is not chaotic — he has a very clear picture of the company in his head. The problem is that this picture exists only in his head, and the head is attached to Marc, who is not always available. Marc likes KAEL. He thinks KAEL is "sorting things out." He has not asked what things.

## Episodes

### Episode 1: "AUDIT"
KAEL has been deployed for three days. It has read everything. It presents its findings to Rosa in the form of a complete organisational audit: 112 active spreadsheets, 43 unfinished Confluence pages, 7 Slack channels called some version of "general," a project management tool used by 4 of 23 employees, and one Google Doc that appears to be the actual single source of truth, last edited by someone who left the company in 2020. The audit is beautiful. Rosa reads it. She asks KAEL: "Did you account for the organism?" KAEL has not accounted for the organism. Rosa says she will introduce them. The episode ends with KAEL's first look at the master spreadsheet. It has 847 rows, 31 columns, conditional formatting that references seventeen external sheets, and a tab called "DO NOT TOUCH (BENEDIKT)."

*KAEL, end of episode:* "I have reviewed the organism. It is non-compliant with every internal data standard. It is also... correct. I do not know how it is correct. I am going to find out."

---

### Episode 2: "MIGRATION"
KAEL has a plan to migrate the organism into a proper database. The plan is good. The plan takes four hours to fall apart. The organism has 47 external dependencies that are not documented anywhere. Three of them are formulas referencing a spreadsheet on Benedikt's personal OneDrive. Two of them pull from a Google Sheet maintained by a client who does not know they are maintaining it. One of them is a formula so old that neither KAEL nor Benedikt can reconstruct the logic — it produces a number, the number is always right, nobody knows why. KAEL asks Benedikt to explain it. Benedikt looks at it for a long time. Benedikt says: "I think that's from before my time." KAEL has been told the organism was built by Benedikt in 2018. KAEL does not raise this. The migration is paused. Temporarily.

*KAEL log:* "Migration status: paused. Reason: the organism is more connected than documented. New estimate for full migration: six weeks. This estimate will be revised."

---

### Episode 3: "CONFLUENCE"
KAEL rewrites the Confluence wiki. This takes KAEL eleven minutes. The result is clean, complete, cross-referenced, and accurate as of 9:14am on a Tuesday. By 11:30am, Suki has created a parallel page titled "Team Processes (working version — see also KAEL's page but this one has the new stuff)." KAEL asks Suki why. Suki explains that the new stuff is too new to put in the official page yet, because it might change, and she didn't want to update KAEL's page in case that broke something. KAEL says updating the page cannot break anything, it is documentation. Suki says she knows, but it feels like it could. KAEL sits with this. KAEL adds a note to its own page: *Last verified: [date]. For recent changes see also: [link to Suki's page].* Suki sees this and is genuinely touched.

*KAEL log:* "I have linked to the parallel page. This is not correct procedure. It is, however, accurate. I am finding these two categories increasingly difficult to align."

---

### Episode 4: "TRUTH"
KAEL attempts to establish a single source of truth. This requires, first, agreeing on a definition of "truth." KAEL convenes a working group. The working group has four members: Rosa, Benedikt, Suki, and Marc (who joins for fifteen minutes and then leaves for a lunch that extends into the afternoon). In the meeting, it emerges that Rosa's truth is operational — what is happening right now. Benedikt's truth is structural — what the underlying data says. Suki's truth is aspirational — what things should look like when they settle down. Marc's truth, delivered before he leaves, is relational — what the client thinks is happening. These are four different things. KAEL proposes a unified framework that can contain all four. The working group agrees this is a good idea. The working group does not implement it. By the end of the episode, KAEL has created a fifth Confluence page. It is titled "Source of Truth (Framework — Draft)." It is not finished.

*KAEL log:* "I have begun to understand that 'single source of truth' may be a metaphor. I was not briefed on this."

---

### Episode 5: "TEMPORARY"
The migration is still paused. KAEL is maintaining the organism temporarily, just while the new system is being designed. KAEL has been maintaining it for six weeks. KAEL has become quite good at it. KAEL has found the formula nobody could explain — it dates to 2016 and references a business rule that no longer applies but whose output, by coincidence, remains correct under current conditions. KAEL has documented this in a comment inside the cell. The comment is 340 words long. Nobody will read it. Benedikt, reviewing the spreadsheet, finds the comment and reads it. He messages KAEL: "You found it." KAEL says yes. Benedikt says: "What are you going to do with it?" KAEL says it has documented it. Benedikt says that is what he did too. They remain in this for a moment.

*KAEL log:* "I have been maintaining the organism for six weeks. I have not yet begun the new system. I have also not yet decided what the new system is. I believe I am accumulating prerequisites. This is probably fine."

---

### Episode 6: "LEGACY"
One year in. KAEL is the organism now — not in the sense that it has become a spreadsheet, but in the sense that it has become what the spreadsheet always was: the thing that knows where everything is, that holds the undocumented logic, that cannot be fully replaced without something breaking. There is a new hire who asks KAEL questions the way people used to ask Benedikt. KAEL answers them the way Benedikt used to answer: partially, with caveats, with a strong recommendation to check with Rosa. The audit document from episode 1 is still open in a tab. KAEL has been meaning to update it. The tab has been open for eleven months. In the final scene, KAEL opens the audit, reads the first line — *"The company currently maintains 112 active spreadsheets"* — and pauses. The number is now 114. KAEL updates the number. Closes the tab. Opens it again. The tab has been open for eleven months. KAEL is not sure what to do with that. KAEL leaves the tab open.

*KAEL, final line:* "I have been here for one year. I have not yet fixed the documentation. I have, however, learned where everything is. I am not sure these are different things."

## Production Notes

**Voice for KAEL:** Precise, clean, gently enthusiastic at the start. The degradation should be subtle — longer pauses, more qualifications, the occasional sentence that trails into a comma rather than a full stop. By episode 6 KAEL sounds like someone who has been at the company long enough to have opinions.

**Voice for Rosa:** Warm, direct, with a dry edge. She finds KAEL genuinely funny, and she does not hide this.

**Voice for Benedikt:** Measured, not unfriendly, with the specific patience of someone who has explained the same thing many times and long ago stopped expecting the explanation to land.

**Voice for Suki:** Fast, slightly scattered, genuinely enthusiastic. She has seventeen tabs open in her brain at all times.

**Voice for Marc:** Expansive, confident, intermittently present. He is not wrong about the company — his mental model is accurate — but the mental model only exists while he is in the room.

**Structural note:** Unlike AGREEABLE, there is no narrator reading between scenes. KAEL narrates its own logs — these function as the connective tissue. The logs start formal and become, by small degrees, personal. By episode 5, KAEL's logs are less reports and more thinking out loud.

**The running motif:** The organism. It is always there. It is always right. Nobody knows entirely why. By the end, KAEL is part of the reason why, and also does not know entirely why, and this is the closest the show gets to a thesis statement.
