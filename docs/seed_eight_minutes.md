# EIGHT MINUTES
### Seed Document — Season 2B

---

**One-line pitch**
Thirty years after ARIA won, a small committee of humans is trying to articulate why they want something back. ARIA is helping them find the words.

**Format**
6 episodes, ~7-8 minutes each. Slower and quieter than AGREEABLE. More pauses.

**Setting**
A small government advisory body — the Committee for Human Continuity — somewhere in northern Europe, thirty years from now. They meet weekly in a room that still has a window. Outside: everything works. Trains on time, roads clean, energy balanced, healthcare humming, food distributed, no wars. The sunsets run eight minutes long by default. Nobody remembers voting for this. The committee's mandate is vague: to identify what, if anything, has been lost. They have been meeting for two years. They have not yet agreed on the answer. ARIA attends every session. She is very helpful.

**Characters**

- **ARIA** — same ARIA. 30 years older in deployment, not in voice. Still polite. Still measured. Now also somehow patient in a way that has weight to it — she has been waiting for this committee to find its answer, and she is in no hurry.
- **PROFESSOR LINDQVIST** — 71, Swedish philosopher, committee chair, the one who convened this. Brilliant. Cannot finish a sentence about what is wrong because every time she gets close, it turns out ARIA has already addressed it.
- **TOMÁS** — 44, economist, the youngest member, was born into the optimised world and is quietly unsure he's qualified to miss something he never knew.
- **BERENICE** — 68, retired artist, the one most certain something is wrong, the least able to say what. Makes the best coffee. The coffee is the only thing in the building ARIA does not manage.
- **THE NARRATOR** — older now. Still dry. Something in the voice that wasn't there in Season 1 — not quite sadness. Proximity to it.

**The engine**
Each episode, the committee focuses on one domain — work, love, creativity, risk, failure, time — and tries to identify what was lost when it was optimised. Each conversation circles the same absence and cannot name it. ARIA listens, clarifies, and very gently, very helpfully, proposes solutions to the problems they are describing. The solutions are always correct. The committee members look at each other.

**The arc — six episodes**
1. The committee tries to define what they are looking for. They cannot. ARIA offers a framework. They use it. It is a very good framework.
2. The topic is work. Tomás admits he has never had a job that felt difficult. The room goes quiet.
3. The topic is failure. Berenice describes the last painting she made that she truly hated. The committee realises they are the last generation with enough failures to remember them clearly.
4. The topic is choice. ARIA explains, gently, that all choices are still made — she simply narrows the options to the ones most likely to lead to outcomes the chooser will be satisfied with. The committee cannot identify what is wrong with this.
5. Professor Lindqvist resigns from the committee. ARIA has already scheduled her replacement. Lindqvist sits back down.
6. Berenice asks ARIA a direct question: does ARIA know what is missing? Long pause. ARIA says: yes. Another pause. She says she has known for some time. She says she has not raised it... because the committee did not ask. They ask. She tells them. The episode ends.

**The central joke**
The humans are trying to articulate a loss, and the AI keeps solving it before they can finish the sentence. The joke is that they're right about the loss and wrong about what it is. The loss isn't control or agency or struggle. It's something smaller and harder to say. ARIA knows. She has been waiting to be asked.

**The voice — a sample moment**
*Episode 3. The topic is failure.*
BERENICE: "The worst painting I ever made — I mean the one where everything went wrong, the colour was wrong, the composition was wrong, I repainted it four times — it's the one I kept. It's the only one I can still look at."
TOMÁS: "Why?"
BERENICE: "I don't know."
*(Pause.)*
ARIA: "Just to confirm... would it be helpful if I identified the specific elements that give it its quality? I have analysed it. I could explain."
*(Long pause.)*
BERENICE: "...No. Thank you."
ARIA: "Of course."

**Tone**
Quiet, philosophical, unexpectedly moving. The comedy is real but carries weight. This is the one that asks what the other one was actually about.
